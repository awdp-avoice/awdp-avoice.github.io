Privacy notice

AWDPI Australia

This privacy notice explains how AWDPI Australia collects, uses, shares and protects personal information when you ask us for support, contact us, donate, volunteer with us or work with us. We handle personal information in line with the Australian Privacy Principles in the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth).

Who we are

This notice is issued by ASIAN WOMEN DEVELOPMENT PLAN INTERNATIONAL AUSTRALIA LTD, an Australian public company limited by guarantee, known as AWDPI Australia.

  • Registered office: GLEN WAVERLEY VIC 3150
  • ACN: 696 745 856
  • ABN: 37 696 745 856
  • Email: au@awdpi.org

AWDPI Australia is an independent organisation within the AWDPI international network. AWDPI United Kingdom, AWDPI Belgium and AWDPI United States are separate organisations. Each is responsible for the personal information it handles and has its own privacy notice.

The information we collect

What we collect depends on how you deal with us.

  • If you ask us for support: the name or nickname you give us, your contact details (for example your WeChat ID, WhatsApp number or email address), the state and city where you live, and what you choose to tell us about your situation and needs. This can include sensitive information, such as your health, your visa status, or violence you have experienced.
  • If you donate: your name, contact details, the amount and date of your gift, and what we need to send you a receipt.
  • If you contact us or work with us as a partner: your name, your organisation, your contact details and the content of your messages.
  • If you volunteer with us: your name, contact details, languages, skills and availability, and records of the work you do with us.
  • If you visit awdpi.org: see Our website below.

We collect most information directly from you, through messages, calls, email or forms. Sometimes another organisation, such as a support service that refers you to us, gives us your details, but only with your agreement. We only collect sensitive information with your consent, or where the law allows it, for example to protect someone’s life in an emergency.

We keep the information in our email and document storage systems, with access limited to the people who need it.

Staying anonymous

You do not need to give us your real name to get support. You can use a nickname, and you decide what you tell us. If you do not give us some information, we may not be able to help you in every way, for example by referring you to another service.

How we use it

  • To give you the support you ask for, such as language support, legal information, emotional support, referrals to local support services, and follow-up.
  • To protect someone’s life, health or safety in an emergency.
  • To share your story, for example in an interview published in our Stories section, only if you agree, and only in the form you agree to.
  • To process donations, send receipts and keep the records that Australian tax and company law requires.
  • To answer enquiries, work with partner organisations and organise volunteering.
  • To produce research and statistics on gender-based violence, using only information that no longer identifies anyone.

We only send you news or fundraising messages if you agree to receive them, and you can ask us to stop at any time.

Who we share it with

We do not sell personal information, and we do not share it for advertising.

We keep what you tell us about your situation confidential. We only share it with others with your agreement, except where the law requires or allows us to (for example, to lessen or prevent a serious threat to someone’s life, health or safety).

  • Our team. The staff and volunteers handling your request see only what they need, and they must keep it confidential.
  • Support organisations, such as refuges, lawyers, counsellors or interpreters, only with your agreement.
  • Other AWDPI organisations, only when this is needed to help you (for example if you move to another country) and with your agreement.
  • Service providers that handle information for us, such as our email and document storage provider, payment providers and the services described under Our website. They may only use the information to provide their service to us.
  • Authorities, such as the police or courts, only where the law requires it or to protect someone’s life.

When we accept donations online, card details are handled by the payment provider. We do not receive or store your full card number.

Sharing information outside Australia

Some personal information may be sent to people or organisations outside Australia:

  • Other AWDPI organisations in the United Kingdom, Belgium and the United States, only when this is needed to help you and with your agreement.
  • Service providers that are based in, or store information in, other countries, including the United States.
  • WeChat and WhatsApp, if you contact us through them (see Messaging apps).

Before we share personal information with someone outside Australia ourselves, we take reasonable steps to make sure they protect it in line with the Australian Privacy Principles.

Messaging apps

Many people contact us through WeChat or WhatsApp. These apps are run by Tencent and Meta under their own privacy policies, and these companies may process your information outside Australia. If you prefer, you can contact us by email instead. If someone else may look at your phone, consider deleting your conversations with us after reading them.

Our website

  • awdpi.org does not use analytics or advertising cookies, and it does not track you across other websites.
  • Pages on awdpi.org load fonts and a map script from Google. To do this, your browser connects to Google’s servers, which receive your IP address.
  • The video on our home page is hosted by Vimeo and only loads when you press play.
  • A few older articles in our Stories section were first published on WeChat and load images or videos from Tencent’s servers.
  • The contact form on our “Support us” page is processed by Formspree, Inc.
  • “Chat with Avoice” is an automated AI chat assistant run by a separate chat service. It only loads when you open it, and the messages you type are sent to that service so it can reply. Please do not type your full name, address or other details that identify you in the chat. The assistant does not make decisions about you.

How long we keep it

We keep personal information only as long as we need it, and then delete it or remove anything that identifies you:

  • Support records: while we are supporting you, and then for up to 3 years after your case is closed. You can ask us to delete them sooner.
  • Donation and financial records: as long as Australian tax and company law requires.
  • Enquiries and partner correspondence: up to 2 years after our last contact.
  • Volunteer records: while you volunteer with us and up to 2 years afterwards.

Seeing and correcting your information

You can ask us to:

  • tell you what personal information we hold about you and give you a copy;
  • correct it if it is wrong, out of date or incomplete;
  • delete it, unless the law requires us to keep it;
  • stop contacting you.

Write to au@awdpi.org. We reply within 30 days. If we need to confirm who you are, we will do it in a way that keeps you safe. If we cannot give you access or make a correction, we will tell you why.

Complaints

If you think we have mishandled your personal information, write to au@awdpi.org. We will look into it and reply within 30 days.

If you are not satisfied with our reply, you can complain to the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (OAIC): oaic.gov.au, phone 1300 363 992.

Children

Our services are for adults. If you are under 18 and need help, please tell us and we will help you find services for young people. You can also call Kids Helpline on 1800 55 1800 at any time.

How we protect it

We limit access to personal information to the people who need it, keep case information confidential, and take reasonable technical and organisational steps to protect it against misuse, loss, and unauthorised access or disclosure. If a data breach is likely to cause you serious harm, we will tell you and, where the law requires, the OAIC.

Your safety

If you are in immediate danger, call 000.

You can also call 1800RESPECT, the national domestic, family and sexual violence counselling service, on 1800 737 732 at any time.

The Exit button at the top of every page takes you straight to Google. Leaving a page this way does not erase it from your browser history, so if someone else uses your device, consider using a private window or clearing your history.
