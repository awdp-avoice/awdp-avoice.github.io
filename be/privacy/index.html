Privacy notice

AWDPI Belgium

This privacy notice explains how AWDPI Belgium collects, uses and protects personal data when you ask us for support, contact us, volunteer with us or work with us. It is written to meet the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and Belgian data protection law.

Who we are

The data controller is Asian Women Development Plan International, an international non-profit association (AISBL) under Belgian law, known as AWDPI Belgium.

  • Registered office: Chée de Roodebeek 206, 1200 Woluwe-Saint-Lambert, Belgium
  • Enterprise number: 1011.855.795 (KBO/BCE), RPM Bruxelles
  • Email: be@awdpi.org
  • Phone and WhatsApp: +32 492096473

AWDPI Belgium is an independent organisation within the AWDPI international network. AWDPI United Kingdom, AWDPI United States and AWDPI Australia are separate organisations. Each is responsible for the personal data it handles and has its own privacy notice.

For any question about this notice or your personal data, write to be@awdpi.org.

The personal data we collect

What we collect depends on how you deal with us.

  • If you ask us for support: the name or nickname you give us, your contact details (for example your WeChat ID, WhatsApp number or email address), the country and city where you live, and what you choose to tell us about your situation and needs. This can include sensitive information, such as your health, your sexual life, your ethnic origin, or violence you have experienced.
  • If you contact us or work with us as a partner: your name, your organisation, your contact details and the content of your messages.
  • If you volunteer with us: your name, contact details, languages, skills and availability, and records of the work you do with us.
  • If you visit awdpi.org: see Our website below.

You do not need to give us your real name to get support. You can use a nickname, and you decide what you tell us.

Why we use your data and our legal basis

  • To give you the support you ask for, such as language support, legal information, psychological support, referrals to shelters, lawyers or other services, and follow-up. Legal basis: your consent (GDPR Article 6(1)(a)) and, for sensitive information, your explicit consent (Article 9(2)(a)).
  • To protect someone’s life or physical safety in an emergency, when we cannot ask for consent. Legal basis: vital interests (Articles 6(1)(d) and 9(2)(c)).
  • To share your story, for example in an interview published in our Stories section, only if you explicitly agree, and only in the form you agree to. Legal basis: your explicit consent (Articles 6(1)(a) and 9(2)(a)).
  • To answer enquiries and work with partner organisations. Legal basis: our legitimate interest in running our organisation and replying to you (Article 6(1)(f)).
  • To organise volunteering. Legal basis: our arrangement with you and our legitimate interest in managing our volunteers (Articles 6(1)(b) and 6(1)(f)).
  • To keep accounts and meet our legal obligations under Belgian law. Legal basis: legal obligation (Article 6(1)(c)).
  • To produce research and statistics on gender-based violence. We only use information that no longer identifies anyone. Legal basis: our legitimate interest in understanding and preventing gender-based violence (Article 6(1)(f)).

You can withdraw your consent at any time by telling us. This does not affect what we did with your consent before you withdrew it.

Who we share it with

We do not sell personal data and we do not use it for advertising.

  • Our team. The staff and volunteers handling your request see only what they need, and they must keep it confidential.
  • Support organisations, such as shelters, lawyers, psychologists or interpreters, only with your agreement, except in an emergency.
  • Other AWDPI organisations, only when this is needed to help you (for example if you move to another country) and with your agreement.
  • Service providers that process data for us, such as our email and document storage provider and the services described under Our website. They may only use the data to provide their service to us.
  • Authorities, such as the police or courts, only where the law requires it or to protect someone’s life.

Messaging apps

Many people contact us through WeChat or WhatsApp. These apps are run by Tencent and Meta under their own privacy policies, and these companies may process your data outside the European Union. If you prefer, you can contact us by email or phone instead. If someone else may look at your phone, consider deleting your conversations with us after reading them.

Our website

  • awdpi.org does not use analytics or advertising cookies.
  • Pages on awdpi.org load fonts and a map script from Google. To do this, your browser connects to Google’s servers, which receive your IP address.
  • The video on our home page is hosted by Vimeo and only loads when you press play.
  • A few older articles in our Stories section were first published on WeChat and load images or videos from Tencent’s servers.
  • The contact form on our “Support us” page is processed by Formspree, Inc. in the United States.
  • “Chat with Avoice” is an automated AI chat assistant run by a separate chat service. It only loads when you open it, and the messages you type are sent to that service so it can reply. Please do not type your full name, address or other details that identify you in the chat. The assistant does not make decisions about you.

Transfers outside the European Economic Area

Some of the providers above process data outside the European Economic Area. When we transfer personal data outside the EEA ourselves, we rely on an adequacy decision of the European Commission or on appropriate safeguards, such as the European Commission’s Standard Contractual Clauses.

How long we keep it

We keep personal data only as long as we need it:

  • Support records: while we are supporting you, and then for up to 3 years after your case is closed, after which we delete or anonymise them. You can ask us to delete them sooner.
  • Enquiries and partner correspondence: up to 2 years after our last contact.
  • Volunteer records: while you volunteer with us and up to 2 years afterwards.
  • Accounting records: as long as Belgian law requires.

Your rights

You have the right to:

  • see the personal data we hold about you and get a copy of it;
  • have it corrected if it is wrong;
  • have it deleted;
  • restrict how we use it, or object to our use of it;
  • receive it in a portable format;
  • withdraw your consent at any time.

To use any of these rights, write to be@awdpi.org. We reply within one month. If we need to confirm who you are, we will do it in a way that keeps you safe.

We do not make decisions about you based solely on automated processing.

If you are not satisfied with how we handle your data, you can complain to the Belgian Data Protection Authority (Autorité de protection des données / Gegevensbeschermingsautoriteit), Rue de la Presse 35, 1000 Brussels, www.dataprotectionauthority.be.

How we protect it

We limit access to personal data to the people who need it, keep case information confidential, and take technical and organisational measures to protect it against loss and misuse.

Your safety

If you are in immediate danger in Belgium, call 112.

The Exit button at the top of every page takes you straight to Google. Leaving a page this way does not erase it from your browser history, so if someone else uses your device, consider using a private window or clearing your history.
