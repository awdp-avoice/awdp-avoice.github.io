Avoice Chinese Women's Global Development Conference

January 20, 2023




Avoice successfully held the first Chinese Women's 

Global Development Conference

On January 7, 2023, the first Avoice Chinese Women's Global Development Conference was successfully held. The forum was co-hosted by Avoice and HaiNan Philanthropy Development Association. The forum aims to build a network for organizations, scholars, and guests dedicated to Chinese women's development, create practical opportunities to engage in gender issues, and further expand the freedoms and civil rights of overseas Chinese women.


Over ten domestic and international experts and scholars from UN Women, international organizations, academic institutions, famous universities, science and technology companies were invited to participate online, with Wiley and Claire, the founders of The Wannabe Feminists podcast, as the hosts. The forum featured three sections: guest speeches, topic-based panels, and a dissertation review. The topic of discussion focused on gender data but dives into a wide-reaching plethora of topics, including technology, public welfare, workplace, education, law, psychology, and policy. The detailed yet broad discourse aimed to spread knowledge across age groups, fields of study, and geographical regions, providing participants with profound and innovative perspectives on anti-gender violence theory and practice.


On the very day, 532 people participated the conference through the Zoom meeting and 3617 people wanted the conference through live streaming platforms. Domestic media such as People's Daily EU and Gangaonet, and web portals including Sohu, NetEase, Tencent, Ifeng News etc, also released news about this successful conference.

At the beginning of the forum, Yimar Yu, the founder of Avoice, shared the current situation of that overseas Chinese women are facing on the road to gender equality. Yimar also presented the mission and vision of the establishment of Avoice to begin the conference.

Session 1: Keynote Speech

Investigating the State of Domestic/Gender-Based Violence and the Legal Situation facing migrant women


In her speech titled "Foreigners, Gender Violence and the Law", Zhang Lining, a UN Women China Fellow and expert in the research group on International Anti-Discrimination Law at the University of California, Berkeley School of Law, mentioned that female migrant groups face many challenges whilst living in foreign lands, including human trafficking, toxic beauty standards, and female stereotypes in the media, which further exacerbate their risk of facing various kinds of violence. Zhang Lining emphasized, "The law cannot stand alone, and we need to work together to create a world with zero tolerance for gender-based violence."

Zhang Lining


Exploring how young women can participate in, and lead, gender projects, using anti-domestic violence missions as an example


In a speech titled "How to Involve Young Women and Lead Gender Projects," Chen Zhenghong, director of the Beijing Women's Theory Research Association's Professional Committee on Traditional Culture and Women's Development, suggested that protecting women is not simply assisting women facing domestic violence, as the activist must clearly understand their position in the rescue mission, have the bravery to make decisions, while also protecting themselves. In the process of promoting specific activities, participants should try to link up with multiple sectors and improve the capacity of dialogue.


Finally, Chen Zhenghong summarized the guidance on how to promote gender projects in a sustainable way. While attempting to protect women, the activist must contact several different departments for assistance, while maintaining transparency and communication between the actors involved


Chen Zhenghong


The Mixed Experiences of Promoting Gender Equality as a Chinese Overseas Woman


Stephanie Wang, Assistant Professor of Gender Studies at St. Lawrence University, presented her speech "Transnational Migration, Gender, and Politics: The Mixed Experience of Chinese Women Overseas”. Stephanie discussed the ways in which gender equality shapes countless lives, and concluded that the road to gender equality is long and difficult, and cannot be bypassed by the success of a few individual women. Then, in a structural gender analysis, Professor Wang explored how different institutional systems exploit women and called for women's rights and gender equality.


Professor Wang said, "Although people may find it difficult to understand others due to difference in identity and class, our task is precisely to connect people at different social levels, discover their commonalities and unite them so that we can see more possibilities."


Stephanie Wang


Session 2: Panel Discussion

Can Internet technology advance gender equality?


Vera Zhang, CEO of China Youth of Tomorrow (CYOT) and curator of TEDx, moderated a panel discussion on "Internet Technology and Gender Equality". Larissa Leng, author of Invisible Faces and founder of NOTINSTUDIO, Sally Shen, Founder of Codopia Education Technology and Key Opinion Leader (KOL) of Web3 Quality Content, and Shi Lan, Founder and CEO of Qafei Technology, were the guests featured in the panel discussing Internet technology and gender.


The three guests analyzed whether the internet has promoted gender equality from the perspectives of hidden gender and data bias and the algorithmic discrimination phenomenon. The panel further discussed how to increase awareness of this gender bias, eliminate hidden bias, and use role models to encourage women to flourish independently despite bias.

How can we promote Asian women to break through barriers in the workplace and broaden their career paths overseas?


The panel was moderated by Guo JiaJia, Founder and CEO of ESG media platform Landing TouchBase. Three quests were invited to speak on the panel: Emily CHEUNG, Co-founder and CEO of Rewardtron, Dr. Polly Ng, Founder and CEO of Global Women Connect, and Yang Qun, Founder of Biorbyt Biotech and first President of the UK Chinese Women's Alliance. The panel discussed how to promote and broaden the overseas career paths of Asian women.


The guests shared their personal experience of discrimination as Chinese women as well as strategies to cope with these obstacles.

The panel advocated for women to improve their self-efficacy, to share parenting responsibilities with their partners, and to have the courage to break traditional gender boundaries and cultural differences to balance career and family with confidence, optimism, and competence.

Session 3: Dissertation Presentation


In order to promote gender research and build an academic platform, the forum invited three distinguished guests to serve as evaluators of the dissertation review. The panel features Han Ling, a postdoctoral fellow at Stanford University's Center for Philanthropy and Social Innovation and an assistant professor at the Gender Studies Program of the Chinese University of Hong Kong, Wang Yanping, director of Avoice's Overseas Chinese Women's Protection Project, and Wang Yingyi, a doctoral fellow in gender studies at the University of Washington and an assistant professor in the Department of Gender Studies at St. Lawrence University. In this part, a total of three presenters shared each of their works related to gender: "Women’s Empowerment Activities under New Media: A Study Based on a Sanitary Napkin Mutual Aid Box Project in a University in Chongqing", "Dangerous Society: A Study on New Dilemmas facing Women Seeking Shelter from Domestic Violence in the COVID-19 pandemic: A Case Study of Beijing", and "A Study on Policy Response to Domestic Violence in Europe after the COVID-19 Epidemic". According to the reviewers, the three papers provide observational studies on topics such as women's health and wellbeing, domestic violence under the epidemic, and the living standards of overseas Chinese women.

In the future, Avoice Chinese Women's Global Development Forum hopes to become a platform for cooperation and discussion to promote the empowerment of Chinese women, create practical opportunities to participate in gender equality initiatives, improve the living standards and strengthen the voices of overseas Chinese women. Avoice looks forward to more participation and more voices joining the effort to promote gender equality to create a welcoming safe space for women!


Avoice Introduction

Avoice's Against Violence to Overseas Chinese Women Program was launched in Belgium in the year 2020 at the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic. This non-profit organization is dedicated to eliminating gender-based violence and promoting gender equality. The program aims to establish an extensive and effective anti-domestic violence network to provide adequate and continuous protection and assistance to overseas Chinese women at risk of domestic violence.


By establishing a network of cooperation with government departments, overseas Chinese organizations, social organizations and industry experts, Avoice unites several advocacy forces to support the plight of overseas Chinese women, create a safe environment for their survival, and help them grow from victims to advocates of gender equality. At present, the project has covered all European countries and some North American countries.
